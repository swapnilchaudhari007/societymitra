# Maharashtra Co-operative Housing Societies — plain-language guide

## Governing law
The Maharashtra Co-operative Societies Act, 1960 governs housing societies in Maharashtra. In 2019 a separate chapter, Chapter XIII-B (Sections 154B-1 to 154B-31), was added that applies specifically to co-operative housing societies. Each society also registers its own bye-laws, usually adopted from the state's Model Bye-laws.

## Annual general body meeting (AGM)
The AGM must be held every year, before 30 September. Members must get written notice of the meeting with the agenda in advance. Accounts, audit report and budget are placed before the AGM. Decisions on maintenance rates, interest on arrears and major repairs are taken by the general body.

## Maintenance charges and how they are split
Typical heads: service charges, property tax, water charges, electricity for common areas, sinking fund, repairs and maintenance fund, lift maintenance, parking charges, non-occupancy charges, insurance.
Service charges are generally shared equally per flat. Property tax, sinking fund and repair fund contributions are generally linked to the flat's area or construction cost.

## Sinking fund and repair fund
Sinking fund contribution is at least 0.25% per year of the construction cost of each flat. Repairs and maintenance fund contribution is at least 0.75% per year of the construction cost of each flat, unless the general body decides otherwise within the bye-laws.

## Interest on late payment
If a member does not pay dues by the due date, the society may charge simple interest at the rate decided by the general body, up to a maximum of 21% per year. Interest is charged on the outstanding amount, not compounded.

## Defaulters and recovery
A member who does not pay dues within three months of the bill is a defaulter. The society must send written notice before action. The society can apply to the Registrar for a recovery certificate (Section 101 / Chapter XIII-B recovery provisions), which is enforced like a decree. A defaulter can lose the right to vote or contest committee elections. The society cannot cut water supply or electricity, or block lift use, to recover dues.

## Non-occupancy charges
When a flat is let out (leave and licence), the society may levy non-occupancy charges up to 10% of the service charges (excluding municipal taxes) per month, as per the Government Resolution of 2001. Charges above this cap are not allowed. Close relatives living in the flat (parents, children, siblings, spouse) are usually not treated as non-occupants.

## Letting out a flat (leave and licence)
A member does not need a "No Objection Certificate" from the society to let out the flat. The member must inform the society in writing, give a copy of the leave and licence agreement and the police verification of the tenant. The member remains responsible for dues.

## Transfer of flat and transfer premium
On sale/transfer, the society may charge a transfer premium of at most Rs 25,000. The society cannot refuse membership to a buyer without valid reasons under the bye-laws, and must decide on an application within the time limits in the bye-laws (deemed membership applies if it does not decide in time).

## Nomination
Every member can nominate one or more persons. The nominee is admitted as a member on the member's death subject to the bye-laws. Nomination is not ownership: legal heirs' rights are decided by succession law.

## Repairs and leakage
The society is responsible for repairs to the structure, terrace, external walls, common pipelines, drainage, compound and common areas. The member is responsible for repairs inside the flat: internal plumbing, fittings, tiles, internal walls and leakage originating inside their own flat. When water leaks from an upper flat's bathroom into the flat below, the member of the upper flat generally bears the repair cost; when the source is the terrace, external wall or a common pipe, the society bears it. The committee should inspect within a reasonable time and record findings in writing.

## Parking
Parking spaces in the society's compound belong to the society; open parking cannot be sold by the builder (Supreme Court, Nahalchand Laloochand v. Panchali CHS, 2010). Allotment is by the managing committee as per the bye-laws, usually first-come-first-served or by lottery/rotation, with charges fixed by the general body.

## Pets
The bye-laws do not allow a society to ban members from keeping pets. The Animal Welfare Board of India advisory (2015) states societies cannot ban pets or levy discriminatory charges for them. Owners must keep common areas clean and prevent nuisance.

## Festival and voluntary contributions
Contributions for festivals or celebrations are voluntary. They cannot be added to the maintenance bill or compelled.

## Complaints by members
A member should give a complaint in writing to the secretary/managing committee. The committee should acknowledge it, discuss it in the next committee meeting and reply in writing. If unresolved, the member may approach the Registrar (Deputy/Assistant Registrar of Co-operative Societies), and for disputes, the Co-operative Court.

## Managing committee
The managing committee runs day-to-day affairs. It must meet regularly (at least once a month is the common bye-law requirement) and keep minutes. Elections are held every five years. Committee members must not have personal financial interest in society contracts.

## Records members can inspect
Members can inspect and get copies of the bye-laws, audited accounts, AGM minutes, and other records as allowed by the Act, on paying the prescribed fee. The society must provide them within the time limit in the bye-laws.

## Redevelopment
Redevelopment decisions require a special general body meeting with a defined quorum and majority as per the Government directives under Section 79A, in the presence of an authorised officer of the Registrar.
